Privacy Policy

Effective date: 31 July 2026 · Applies to: IOKey for Android (com.localboard.keyboard) · Contact: support@ioservices.io

IOKey does not collect anything. Nothing you type is sent anywhere.

There is no account, no sign-in, no analytics, no advertising, no crash reporting service, and no server that belongs to us — we do not operate a backend at all. Everything the keyboard learns about you stays in encrypted storage on your own phone and is deleted when you uninstall the app.

A keyboard sees every password, message and search you type. That is why this app is built so that the data physically cannot leave: not as a promise, but because there is nowhere for it to go.

1. What we collect

Nothing. We operate no servers and receive no data from the app — not usage statistics, not diagnostics, not your device identifier, not a count of installs beyond what Google Play reports to every developer.

We cannot identify you, and we could not hand over your typing data if we were asked for it, because we have never had it.

2. What the app stores on your device

All of this lives only on your phone, in the app's private storage. Other apps cannot read it.

Encryption. The typing database is encrypted with SQLCipher (AES-256). The key is generated on your device, stored in the Android Keystore — hardware-backed on phones that have a secure element — and never leaves it. It is not derived from anything we know, and we have no copy.

Deleting it

  • Settings → Data controls lets you erase the learned dictionary and typing history at any time, and inspect exactly what has been learned before you do.
  • Uninstalling the app destroys all of it, including the encryption key.

There is no server-side copy to request the deletion of, because there is no server.

3. When the app uses the network

The app declares the INTERNET permission and uses it for exactly two things, both of which you start deliberately:

  1. Downloading an optional AI language model that you pick in Settings → Language model. The download goes to Hugging Face (huggingface.co). The request contains no personal data — it is an anonymous file download, like any other. Once the file is on your phone the model runs entirely offline.
  2. Downloading an optional offline voice-typing model, from the same place and on the same terms.

Nothing else ever goes out. Specifically: your keystrokes, your dictionary, your messages, your clipboard and your settings are never transmitted, not even in aggregated or anonymised form.

Two controls let you verify this rather than trust it:

  • The network kill switch (Settings → Data controls) blocks the app from opening any connection at all. The keyboard is fully functional with it on.
  • The egress ledger records every network request the app makes, with what it was for, so you can audit the claim above for yourself.

Some permissions are added automatically by Google's own libraries (for example a wake lock and boot-completed receiver used by the scheduler that improves your predictions overnight while the phone is charging). They are not used to send anything.

IOKey never asks for location, camera, photos, files, call logs, or device identifiers, because it has no use for them.

5. Incognito and per-app rules

When an app asks Android for a private text field — a password box, or a browser in private-browsing mode — IOKey honours it and learns nothing from what you type there.

You can also set your own rules per app in Settings → Data controls: pick any app and tell the keyboard never to learn from it.

6. Payments

The Pro unlock is a one-time purchase, not a subscription.

The transaction is handled entirely by Google Play Billing. We never see or receive your card number, billing address, or any other payment detail — Google tells the app only whether a purchase for this account exists.

Once bought, the unlock is remembered locally on the device. The app does not phone home to re-check it, which means Pro keeps working in aeroplane mode and keeps working if we ever disappear.

Google's handling of the payment is covered by Google's Privacy Policy.

7. Children

IOKey is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them. Since the app collects no information from anyone, there is nothing for us to hold or delete.

8. Your rights (GDPR, CCPA and similar)

Laws like the GDPR and CCPA give you the right to access, correct, export and delete the personal data a company holds about you, and to know who it has been sold or shared with.

We hold no personal data about you, and we sell and share none, so there is nothing to request from us. The equivalent controls are in the app itself and work instantly and offline:

  • Access — Settings → My writing style shows everything the keyboard has learned.
  • Export — Settings → Data controls exports it to an encrypted file you control.
  • Deletion — the same screen erases it, or uninstall the app.

If you are in the EEA or UK, the legal basis for the on-device processing described above is your consent, given by installing and enabling the keyboard and by turning on each optional feature individually. Withdrawing it is done by turning the feature off.

9. The direct-download version

This section does not apply to the Google Play version of IOKey. The two capabilities below are absent from the Play build — the permission is not requested and the code is not included in the app at all.

A separate build of IOKey is distributed directly (outside the Play Store) with two extra, opt-in capabilities:

  • Writing-style import from SMS. If you explicitly choose it during setup, the app makes one pass over messages you have sent to learn your writing style. It reads them on the device, records only style statistics, and never transmits or stores the message text.
  • On-screen context for AI drafting. An Android accessibility service that, once you switch it on in your system Accessibility settings, lets the AI see the conversation on screen so a reply it drafts makes sense. The text is held in memory only, never written to disk, and never transmitted. It is off until you enable it and stops the moment you turn it off.

Both are optional, both are off by default, and neither sends anything anywhere.
